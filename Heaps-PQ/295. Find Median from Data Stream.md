# 295. Find Median from Data Stream

## Pattern

Two Heaps

## Intuition

We need the median of a continuously growing stream.

Instead of sorting every time, split the numbers into two halves:

   Left Half       Right Half
   Max Heap        Min Heap

The left Max Heap gives us the largest value in the smaller half.

The right Min Heap gives us the smallest value in the larger half.

These two boundary values are enough to determine the median.

## Approach

1. Maintain two heaps:

   `left` → Max Heap containing the smaller half.

   `right` → Min Heap containing the larger half.

2. When adding a number:
   - If it belongs to the smaller half, put it in `left`.
   - Otherwise put it in `right`.

3. Keep the heaps balanced:
   - `left` can have at most one more element than `right`.
   - `right` should never have more elements than `left`.

4. If `left` becomes too large:
   - Move its maximum element to `right`.

5. If `right` becomes larger:
   - Move its minimum element to `left`.

6. Find the median:
   - Equal sizes → average of both heap tops.
   - `left` has one extra → `left.top()` is the median.

## Time Complexity

`addNum` → O(log n)

`findMedian` → O(1)

## Space Complexity

O(n)

- Both heaps together store all numbers.

## Interview Note

- Think **"Two halves → two heaps."**
- Max Heap = smaller half.
- Min Heap = larger half.
- Keep sizes balanced.
- Median is always at the boundary between the two heaps.
- Don't sort after every insertion.

## Mental Model

For stream:

   1, 2, 3, 4, 5

After processing:

   Left:  [1, 2, 3]
   Right: [4, 5]

Left is a Max Heap:

   top = 3

Right is a Min Heap:

   top = 4

For odd count:

   median = 3

For:

   1, 2, 3, 4

We have:

   Left:  [1, 2]
   Right: [3, 4]

Median:

   (2 + 3) / 2 = 2.5

## Key Idea

**Max Heap → smaller half**

**Min Heap → larger half**

**Balance them → median is immediately available.**

This gives O(log n) insertion and O(1) median lookup.
